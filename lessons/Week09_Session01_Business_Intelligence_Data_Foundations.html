Data hierarchy

From raw facts to actionable wisdom

SCRIPT: "Let's start with the most fundamental distinction: data is not information, information is not knowledge, and knowledge is not wisdom. Each level requires human or system processing."
DIKW Pyramid

Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom

Data

Raw facts without context. Example: "₱45,000" or "2026-07-15"

Information

Data with context and meaning. Example: "July 15 sales were ₱45,000, which is 20% above average."

Knowledge

Information combined with experience. Example: "Sales spike because of payday week patterns."

Wisdom

Judgment for action. Example: "Stock more inventory before payday weeks."

SCRIPT: "As accountants, we often work at the information and knowledge layers. We take raw transaction data and produce meaningful financial statements and analyses. BI tools help automate the transformation from data to information. But knowledge and wisdom still require human understanding of the business."
Data Types

Structured, semi-structured, unstructured

Structured

Organized in tables with defined fields. Example: database transactions, spreadsheets, ERP records.

Semi-structured

Has some organization but flexible format. Example: JSON, XML, emails with attachments, invoices.

Unstructured

No predefined model. Example: contracts, images, audio recordings, social media posts.

For accountants: Most financial data is structured. But supporting evidence (contracts, emails, receipts) is often semi-structured or unstructured. AI helps bridge this gap.

SCRIPT: "Sa accounting, structured data ang pinaka-common: journal entries, trial balance, subsidiary ledgers. Pero ang supporting documents like contracts, bank statements as images, scanned receipts — those are semi-structured or unstructured. This is where OCR and AI become useful."
Data Quality

Six dimensions of data quality

DimensionQuestionAccounting example
CompletenessIs all required data present?Are all invoices recorded?
AccuracyDoes data reflect reality?Are amounts correct?
ConsistencyDoes data agree across systems?Do subsidiary and GL match?
TimelinessIs data available when needed?Is data current for reporting?
ValidityDoes data conform to rules?Are account codes valid?
UniquenessAre there duplicates?Is the same invoice entered twice?
SCRIPT: "Data quality dimensions are your checklist for trusting any report or dashboard. If the source data fails on completeness or accuracy, no amount of beautiful visualization will fix the problem. Garbage in, garbage out — applies to BI just as it applies to financial statements."
Data Governance

Who owns the data?

Data Owner

Business person accountable for data quality and access decisions.

Data Steward

Day-to-day management of data quality, definitions, and standards.

Data Consumer

Uses data for analysis, reporting, or decisions.

Data Custodian

IT person managing technical storage, security, and backups.

Policies

Rules for classification, retention, sharing, and privacy.

Standards

Naming, formats, codes, and documentation requirements.

SCRIPT: "Data governance answers: who decides what data means, who can access it, how long we keep it, and how we ensure quality. Without governance, different departments may define 'revenue' differently — leading to conflicting reports. The accountant is often the natural data steward for financial information."

02
Business Intelligence

From transaction systems to decision-support dashboards

SCRIPT: "BI is the technology and process of collecting, integrating, analyzing, and presenting business data to support better decisions. It is not new — but modern BI tools make it accessible to non-programmers."
Definition

What is Business Intelligence?

BI is the combination of tools, technologies, and practices for collecting, integrating, analyzing, and presenting business data to support organizational decision-making.

Simple version: BI answers "What happened?" and "Why?" using data visualizations that update automatically from your business systems.

SCRIPT: "BI is not one product. It is a category. Power BI, Tableau, Google Looker, Metabase — all BI tools. They connect to your data sources, transform data, and present it in interactive dashboards. The goal: better decisions, faster."
BI Architecture

How data flows from source to dashboard

LayerFunctionExample
Source systemsGenerate transactionsPOS, ERP, bank, payroll
ETL / IntegrationExtract, Transform, Load dataScheduled data pipelines
Data warehouseStore clean, integrated dataCentral analytical database
Analytics layerCalculate measures and modelsDAX formulas, SQL queries
PresentationVisualize for usersDashboards, reports, alerts
DecisionHuman action based on insightAdjust strategy, investigate
SCRIPT: "Think of it as a pipeline. Source systems produce raw data. ETL cleans and integrates it. The warehouse stores it in a query-friendly format. The analytics layer calculates KPIs. The dashboard presents it. And finally, a human decides. Each layer can introduce errors — that's why data quality matters throughout."

Dashboards

What makes a good dashboard?

  • Purpose-driven: answers specific questions for a defined audience
  • Real-time or near real-time: data refreshes automatically
  • Visual hierarchy: most important KPIs largest and first
  • Actionable: tells the viewer what to investigate or decide
  • Trustworthy: shows data source, last refresh, and definitions
  • Interactive: filters, drill-down, and time comparisons
SCRIPT: "A dashboard is not a decoration. It is a decision tool. If a manager looks at it and cannot determine what action to take, it has failed. Good dashboards answer: Are we on track? Where are the problems? What changed?"
Accounting KPIs

Key metrics for financial dashboards

Revenue & Profitability

Revenue trend, gross margin, net income, revenue by segment/branch

Cash & Liquidity

Cash position, cash flow forecast, days sales outstanding, aging

Expenses

Operating expenses vs budget, cost per unit, expense categories

Operational

Invoice cycle time, close completion, exception rates, overdue items

SCRIPT: "These are the KPIs you'll build dashboards for. Revenue tells management if the business is growing. Cash tells them if they can pay bills. Expenses show efficiency. Operational metrics show process health. As AIS professionals, YOU define what these numbers mean and ensure they're calculated correctly."
The Four Analytics Levels

From looking back to looking forward

LevelQuestionMethodExample
DescriptiveWhat happened?Reports, dashboardsQ2 revenue was ₱4.5M
DiagnosticWhy did it happen?Drill-down, varianceBranch 3 declined due to closure
PredictiveWhat will happen?Forecasting, MLCash will be tight in August
PrescriptiveWhat should we do?Optimization, AIAccelerate collections from top 5 clients
SCRIPT: "Most organizations are still mostly at the descriptive level. Some have diagnostic capability. Very few do predictive and prescriptive well. Your competitive advantage as a modern accountant is moving UP these levels — not just reporting what happened, but explaining why and predicting what's next."
ETL Process

Extract, Transform, Load: the backbone of BI

  • Extract: Pull data from source systems (POS, bank, ERP, spreadsheets)
  • Transform: Clean, standardize, calculate, and map to common definitions
  • Load: Store the transformed data in a warehouse or dataset
  • Schedule: Automate the pipeline to run daily, hourly, or on trigger
  • Monitor: Detect failures, quality issues, and volume changes

Accountant's role: Define transformation rules — how revenue is calculated, what gets excluded, how currencies convert, where cutoffs apply.

SCRIPT: "ETL is where accountants ADD massive value. The transform step is where business rules live. How is revenue recognized? What's included in operating expenses? How are intercompany transactions eliminated? These are accounting decisions embedded in data pipelines."
Data Warehouse

A single source of truth

What it is

A central, integrated, historical database designed for analysis and reporting — separate from operational systems.

Why it matters

Prevents conflicting reports. Everyone uses the same definitions, same calculations, same data. One version of truth.

Star schema

Fact tables (transactions) connected to dimension tables (time, account, branch, customer). Optimized for queries.

For accountants

The warehouse should reflect accounting standards, chart of accounts, and management reporting dimensions.

SCRIPT: "Without a data warehouse, different departments query different systems and get different answers. 'What was our revenue last month?' should have ONE answer. The warehouse provides that. As accountants, you help design the structure — especially the dimensions like time periods, account hierarchy, and reporting segments."
Self-Service BI

When accountants build their own reports

  • Benefit: Faster insights without waiting for IT
  • Tools: Power BI Desktop, Google Sheets pivot, Looker Studio
  • Risk: Inconsistent definitions, wrong calculations, ungoverned data
  • Governance: Certified datasets, approved definitions, review before publishing
  • Skill needed: Data modeling, DAX/formulas, visualization principles
SCRIPT: "Self-service BI is powerful but dangerous without governance. If 10 accountants each define 'net revenue' differently, the organization has 10 truths. Solution: certified datasets with approved definitions, and a review process before dashboards go to management."
Activity

Design a financial dashboard (on paper)

  • Choose a business: café, retail store, or tuna exporter
  • Define the audience: owner, branch manager, or finance team
  • Select 5-6 KPIs most important for that audience
  • Sketch a layout: what's biggest? What uses a chart vs. number?
  • Identify data sources for each KPI
  • Note one data quality risk per source
SCRIPT: "Groups of 3-4. Sketch on paper. The layout should have visual hierarchy — the most important number should be the largest. Think about what the audience needs to DECIDE, not just what data is available."
Local Context

BI adoption in the Philippines

  • Large enterprises (banks, telcos, BPO) use Power BI and Tableau extensively
  • SMEs mostly use Excel/Google Sheets for analytics
  • Government: DICT promoting data-driven governance, open data portals
  • Challenge: data silos, low data literacy, budget constraints
  • Opportunity: affordable cloud BI tools make analytics accessible to SMEs
SCRIPT: "Sa Philippines, the big companies are data-driven already. BDO, Globe, PLDT — they have full BI teams. But SMEs? Still Excel-dependent. That's an opportunity for you. If you can set up a Power BI dashboard for a small business, you're providing enterprise-level insight at SME cost."
Recap

Key lessons

  • Data quality (completeness, accuracy, consistency) is the foundation of all analytics
  • BI transforms raw data into visual, interactive decision support
  • Architecture: Source → ETL → Warehouse → Analytics → Dashboard → Decision
  • Four analytics levels: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive
  • Accountants define business rules, ensure data quality, and interpret results
  • Self-service BI needs governance to prevent conflicting truths
