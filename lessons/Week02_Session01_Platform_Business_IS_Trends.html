The Platform
Economy

Business models built on connections, not inventory

Definition

What is a Platform Business?

A platform business creates value by facilitating exchanges between two or more interdependent groups, usually consumers and producers.

Key Principle: Platforms don't OWN the means of production. They own the NETWORK and the DATA.

SCRIPT: "Unsa ang Platform Business? Simple: it's a business that creates value by CONNECTING groups — buyers and sellers, riders and drivers, hosts and travelers. Ang key difference from traditional businesses: platforms DON'T OWN the inventory. Grab doesn't own cars. Shopee doesn't own products. Airbnb doesn't own properties. What they own is the NETWORK — the connections — ug ang DATA. And that, class, is incredibly powerful. Because networks grow exponentially — the more users join, the more valuable the platform becomes. This is called NETWORK EFFECTS."

SCRIPT: "Let's compare Pipeline vs Platform. Traditional businesses — like Jollibee or SM — they MAKE or BUY products, then sell directly. Linear process: produce → distribute → sell. That's a pipeline. Platform businesses FACILITATE exchanges. They don't make the product. They connect buyers and sellers. Scaling: traditional kailangan og more inventory, more stores — expensive. Platforms? More users costs almost nothing — low marginal cost! Revenue: traditional sells products. Platforms earn through commissions, subscriptions, ads. Competitive advantage: traditional relies on supply chain. Platforms rely on NETWORK EFFECTS — the more users, the harder to compete with. Class, this is why tech companies are the most valuable in the world now."

Network Effects

The Power of Network Effects

Example: GCash with 1,000 users = almost useless. GCash with 86M users = indispensable. The VALUE grows exponentially with users.

SCRIPT: "Ang most powerful concept sa platform business is NETWORK EFFECTS. Meaning: the more users join, the more VALUABLE the platform becomes for EVERYONE. Think about it — GCash with 1,000 users, useful ba? Dili kaayo, kay kinsa man ang padalhan nimo? But GCash with 86 million users? Indispensable! Everyone uses it, every store accepts it. That's a FLYWHEEL: more users attract more merchants, more merchants attract more users, cycle na. Once you hit critical mass, very hard na for competitors to catch up. That's why platforms tend toward MONOPOLY or OLIGOPOLY — winner takes most. This is important for business analysis ug valuation."

  • Strategy: Become the "everyday everything app" for SE Asia
  • Revenue: Commission + delivery fees + GrabPay + ads
  • Data Power: Knows where you go, what you eat, when you travel
SCRIPT: "Let's deep dive into Grab. Started in 2012 as simple ride-hailing sa Malaysia. But look at it now — rides, food delivery, package delivery, payments (GrabPay), insurance, investments. They want to be ang 'everyday everything app' for Southeast Asia. Revenue? Commission sa rides ug food orders, delivery fees, GrabPay transaction fees, advertising. But ang real power? DATA. Grab knows where you go, what time, what you eat, how much you spend. That data is incredibly valuable for personalization, for partnerships, for business intelligence. As accountants, how do you value a company like Grab? Traditional methods dili applicable. You need to understand platform economics."
Case: Shopee

Shopee: Marketplace + Ecosystem

Platform Components

• Marketplace (buyer-seller matching)
• ShopeePay (fintech/payments)
• Shopee Express (logistics)
• Shopee Ads (advertising)
• Shopee Live (social commerce)
• Shopee Mall (brand stores)

How Shopee Makes Money

• Seller commission: 2-5% per transaction
• Shopee Ads: sellers pay for visibility
• ShopeePay fees: transaction charges
• Logistics margins: Shopee Express
• Coin cashback: drives repeat purchase
• Data insights: sold to brands

IS Impact: Shopee's success depends entirely on its Information Systems — recommendation algorithms, logistics optimization, real-time inventory, fraud detection.

SCRIPT: "Shopee — everyone uses Shopee, diba? But have you analyzed it as a BUSINESS SYSTEM? Look at the components. It's not just a marketplace — it's an ECOSYSTEM. They have their own payment system (ShopeePay), logistics (Shopee Express), advertising platform (Shopee Ads), live streaming (Shopee Live), ug brand stores (Shopee Mall). Each component generates revenue differently. And here's the IS connection: ALL of this runs on sophisticated Information Systems. Recommendation algorithms suggest products. Logistics AI optimizes delivery routes. Real-time inventory tracking prevents overselling. Fraud detection AI blocks fake sellers. Without IS, Shopee would collapse immediately. THAT'S the power of Information Systems in modern business."

AI in Accounting

How AI is Transforming Accounting

  • Automated data entry: OCR reads receipts/invoices automatically
  • Anomaly detection: AI flags unusual transactions for auditors
  • Predictive analytics: Forecast cash flow, revenue, expenses
  • Tax optimization: AI finds legal tax-saving opportunities
  • Natural language queries: "Show me Q3 expenses over budget"
SCRIPT: "AI sa accounting — five key areas. First, AUTOMATED DATA ENTRY — OCR (Optical Character Recognition) reads receipts ug invoices, automatically encodes. Second, ANOMALY DETECTION — AI reviews ALL transactions ug flags ang unusual ones para sa auditors. Third, PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS — forecast cash flow, when will we run out of money? Fourth, TAX OPTIMIZATION — AI finds legal ways to reduce tax burden. Fifth, NATURAL LANGUAGE QUERIES — instead of building complex reports, you just ask 'Show me Q3 expenses over budget' ug the AI generates it. Now, will AI replace accountants? NO. But accountants who USE AI will replace those who don't. Big difference."

"What happened?"
Financial reports, KPIs, dashboards showing past performance

Diagnostic Analytics

"Why did it happen?"
Variance analysis, drill-down reports, root cause identification

Predictive Analytics

"What will happen?"
Cash flow forecasting, credit risk scoring, demand prediction

Prescriptive Analytics

"What should we do?"
AI-recommended actions, optimal pricing, resource allocation

SCRIPT: "As accountants, data analytics is your NEW superpower. Four levels. DESCRIPTIVE: What happened? Traditional reports — revenue was X, expenses were Y. Most accounting stops here. DIAGNOSTIC: Why did it happen? Variance analysis, drill-down into details. PREDICTIVE: What WILL happen? Forecasting future cash flows, predicting which customers won't pay. PRESCRIPTIVE: What SHOULD we do? AI recommends actions — 'Raise prices by 5% for this segment.' Class, traditional accountants do levels 1-2. Modern accountants do ALL FOUR. That's your competitive advantage. We'll dive deeper into analytics tools in Module 3."

Virtual replicas of physical objects/processes for simulation and optimization before real-world implementation.
