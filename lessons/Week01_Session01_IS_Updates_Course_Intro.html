What are
Information Systems?

A quick review of fundamentals before we dive into updates

Fundamentals

Information Systems Defined

An Information System (IS) is an organized combination of people, hardware, software, communication networks, data resources, and policies that stores, retrieves, transforms, and disseminates information in an organization.

IS is not just technology — it's the entire ecosystem that supports decision-making.

SCRIPT: "So let's start with the basics. Unsa gyud ba ang Information System? Simply put, it's a combination of people, hardware, software, networks, data, and policies nga nag-work together para mag-collect, store, process, ug distribute sa information. Important ni: IS is not just about computers or software. It includes ang PEOPLE, ang processes, ang policies. Tan-awa ninyo ang picture — ang dashboard sa screen represents output sa IS, pero behind that, daghan og components nga nag-collaborate."
Core Components

The 5 Components of Information Systems

1. Hardware

Physical devices: computers, servers, mobile devices, networking equipment

2. Software

Programs & applications: OS, ERP, databases, cloud apps

3. Data

Raw facts & figures transformed into useful information

4. People

Users, IT staff, managers who interact with the system

5. Processes

Policies, procedures, and rules governing IS operations

+ Networks

Communication infrastructure connecting all components

SCRIPT: "Ang IS naay 5 main components — plus networks as the connector. Una, HARDWARE — physical devices like computers, servers. Ikaduha, SOFTWARE — ang programs nga nag-run sa hardware. Ikatulo, DATA — raw facts nga gi-process para mahimong information. Ikaupat, PEOPLE — tanan ang nag-interact sa system, gikan users hangtud IT professionals. Ikalima, PROCESSES — ang rules ug procedures. Ug finally, NETWORKS — ang infrastructure nga nag-connect sa tanan. Kining tanan, mag-work together sila. Kung usa ra ang missing, dili mag-function ang IS properly."
Historical Context

The Evolution of Information Systems

1950s-1960s Mainframe Era — Batch processing, large centralized computers
1970s-1980s PC Revolution — Personal computers, spreadsheets, local databases
1990s Internet & Client-Server — Web, email, networked systems, ERP
2000s-2010s Cloud & Mobile — SaaS, smartphones, social media, big data
2020s AI & Automation — Generative AI, ML, IoT, blockchain, digital twins
SCRIPT: "Let's trace kung giunsa pag-evolve ang IS over the decades. Sa 1950s-60s, mainframes ang hari. Dako kaayo nga computers sa usa ka room. By the 70s-80s, personal computers na — nagka accessible. Then sa 90s, the internet changed everything — email, web, ERP systems. 2000s brought us cloud computing ug mobile. Ug karon, 2020s, we're in the era of AI and automation. Notice class, every decade, mas faster ang change. Ang question karon: unsa ang next? Mao na ang i-explore nato sa tibuok semester."
Comparison

Traditional IS vs. Modern IS

Traditional IS

  • • On-premise servers
  • • Manual data entry
  • • Siloed departments
  • • Paper-based workflows
  • • Reactive decision-making
  • • High upfront costs

Modern IS

  • • Cloud-based infrastructure
  • • Automated data capture
  • • Integrated systems (APIs)
  • • Digital workflows
  • • Predictive analytics & AI
  • • Pay-as-you-go (SaaS)
SCRIPT: "I-compare nato ang Traditional vs Modern IS. Sa traditional, naa tay on-premise servers — kailangan mo og server room, manual data entry, siloed departments. Karon sa modern IS, cloud-based na tanan. Automated na ang data capture. Integrated na ang systems through APIs — meaning, ang accounting system makig-communicate na sa inventory system automatically. Instead of reactive decisions, naa na tay predictive analytics. Ug instead of dagkong upfront costs, pay-as-you-go na lang through SaaS subscriptions. Big difference, diba?"

More than technology — it's a fundamental shift in how organizations operate

Definition

What is Digital Transformation?

Digital Transformation (DT) is the process of using digital technologies to create new or modify existing business processes, culture, and customer experiences to meet changing business and market requirements.

Key Insight: DT is NOT just buying new software. It's about rethinking your entire business model, processes, and culture through the lens of digital possibilities.

SCRIPT: "So unsa gyud ang Digital Transformation? Daghan og misconception about this. People think, 'Oh, nag-install mi og new software, digitally transformed na mi.' WRONG. Digital Transformation is a FUNDAMENTAL rethinking of how your organization operates. It's about using technology to reimagine your business processes, your customer experience, even your company culture. Like for example, ang traditional bank nga nag-switch to mobile banking — that's not just adding an app. They had to rethink everything: security, customer service, processes. THAT'S digital transformation."
Framework

Four Pillars of Digital Transformation

1. Process Transformation

Reimagining workflows with automation, AI, and data analytics for efficiency

2. Business Model Transformation

Shifting from products to services, platforms, subscriptions

3. Domain Transformation

Entering new markets enabled by technology (e.g., Amazon → AWS)

4. Cultural Transformation

Building a digital-first mindset across the entire organization

SCRIPT: "Digital Transformation naay four pillars. First, PROCESS Transformation — pag-automate ug pag-reimagine sa inyong workflows. Second, BUSINESS MODEL Transformation — like kung gikan ka sa selling products, mag-shift ka to subscription model. Third, DOMAIN Transformation — like ang Amazon, gikan bookseller, nag-enter sila sa cloud computing through AWS — totally different domain! Fourth, CULTURAL Transformation — building a digital-first mindset. This is actually the hardest one kay people resist change. Remember: technology is the easy part. Ang challenge gyud is ang people ug culture."

Technology Adoption Lifecycle

SCRIPT: "Kini ang Technology Adoption Lifecycle ni Everett Rogers. Bell curve ang shape. Sa pinakauna, Innovators — 2.5% lang sa population, sila ang first movers. Then Early Adopters — 13.5%, they see the potential early. Early Majority — 34%, they adopt once proven. Late Majority — 34%, they adopt when everyone else does.
