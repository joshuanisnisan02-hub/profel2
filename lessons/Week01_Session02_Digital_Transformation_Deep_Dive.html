How organizations measure their digital readiness

Framework

What is a DT Maturity Model?

A Digital Transformation Maturity Model is a framework that helps organizations assess where they are in their digital journey and what steps to take next.

Think of it like a GPS: You need to know WHERE you are before you can plan WHERE to go.

SCRIPT: "Unsa man ang DT Maturity Model? Simply put, it's like a GPS for organizations. Before ka mag-drive, kailangan nimo mahibal-an asa ka karon, diba? Same thing sa DT — you need to assess your current state before planning your digital future. Ang maturity model gives you STAGES — levels nga mo-indicate kung unsa na ka-advanced ang digital transformation sa isa ka organization. From beginner to fully digital."

Technology Adoption
Strategies

How organizations decide what to adopt and when

Strategy

Big Bang vs. Incremental Approach

  • Big Bang: Replace everything at once. High risk, high reward, fast transformation
  • Incremental: Step-by-step adoption. Lower risk, slower but more manageable
  • Hybrid: Big bang for core systems, incremental for peripherals

Best Practice: Most successful DT projects use the incremental approach with clear milestones.

SCRIPT: "When organizations decide to adopt new technology, naay duha ka main approach. Big Bang — change everything at once. Like kung mag-switch ka from manual to fully automated in one go. High risk ni pero kung successful, fast ang transformation. Then Incremental — step by step, module by module. Lower risk, mas manageable. Most experts recommend incremental approach with clear milestones. Why? Because the failure rate of Big Bang is very high — 70%! Pero kung incremental, you can learn, adjust, ug recover from mistakes. In your career, kung makig-advise mo sa clients, almost always recommend incremental."

SCRIPT: "How do organizations decide WHAT to adopt? Dili lang basta 'trending ni, let's use it.' Naay criteria. First, BUSINESS VALUE — does it actually solve a real problem? What's the return on investment? Second, COST — afford ba nato? Naay tao ba nato nga capable? Third, INTEGRATION — mag-work ba ni with our existing systems or kailangan i-replace tanan? Fourth, SCALABILITY — kung mu-grow ang business, mu-grow pud ba ang tool? Fifth, SECURITY — safe ba? Compliant sa laws? Sixth, VENDOR STABILITY — naa pa ba ni nga company in 5 years? These questions, critical ni for decision-making. As future accountants, you might be involved in these technology investment decisions."

Case Studies:
Success Stories

Learning from organizations that got it right

Success Case #1

Globe Telecom: From Telco to Digital Platform

  • Before: Traditional telecom, voice & text revenue declining
  • Strategy: Transform into digital services platform
  • Actions: GCash, GLife, digital content, fintech
  • Result: GCash reached 86M+ users, fintech now bigger revenue than traditional telco

Key Lesson: Don't cling to your old business model. Create entirely new revenue streams through digital.

SCRIPT: "Let's start with success stories. Globe Telecom — ang atong local telco giant. Before, revenue nila mostly voice calls ug text. But they saw the writing on the wall — declining ang revenue sa traditional telecom. So what did they do? They transformed into a DIGITAL PLATFORM. They invested in GCash — karon 86 million users na! They created GLife for lifestyle services. Ang fintech revenue nila karon, mas dako pa sa traditional telecom revenue! That's domain transformation in action. Lesson: Don't cling to your old business model. If Globe stayed as a traditional telco, nag-decline na sila. Instead, they reinvented themselves."
Success Case #2

BDO: Digital Banking Transformation

  • Challenge: Millions of unbanked Filipinos, physical branches expensive
  • Solution: BDO Online & Mobile App, digital onboarding
  • Integration: Connected to GCash, Maya, merchants
  • Impact: 10M+ digital users, reduced branch dependence by 40%

Key Lesson: Digital channels expand reach without proportional cost increase.

SCRIPT: "Second success story: BDO, largest bank sa Philippines. Their challenge: millions of unbanked Filipinos ug expensive kaayo ang mag-maintain og physical branches. Solution? Invest heavily in BDO Online ug Mobile App. Digital onboarding — pwede na mag-open og account without going to the branch. Nag-integrate sila sa GCash, Maya, merchants. Result? 10 million+ digital users ug 40% less dependence sa physical branches. Lesson: digital channels allow you to serve MORE customers at LOWER cost. For AIS students, this is critical — ang accounting systems ni BDO had to be completely redesigned to support digital operations."
Success Case #3

Netflix: The Ultimate DT Success Story

  • 1997: DVD-by-mail service (physical logistics)
  • 2007: Pivoted to streaming (cloud-based)
  • 2013: Started producing original content using DATA
  • 2024: 280M+ subscribers worldwide, AI-driven recommendations

Key Lesson: Netflix disrupted THEMSELVES before competitors could. They killed their own cash cow (DVDs) to build something better.

SCRIPT: "Now, international case — Netflix. In 1997, they were a DVD-by-mail company. Imagine, nagpadala sila og physical DVD sa bahay mo. But they saw the future: streaming. In 2007, nag-pivot sila — invested heavily in cloud infrastructure para mag-stream. Then in 2013, nag-start sila og original content — ug they used DATA analytics to decide kung unsa ang i-produce. House of Cards? Data said it would be a hit. And it was! Karon, 280 million+ subscribers worldwide. The KEY lesson: Netflix disrupted THEMSELVES. They killed their own profitable DVD business BEFORE competitors could kill it for them. That takes courage. That's true DT leadership."

Case Studies:
Failure Stories

Learning from organizations that got it wrong

Failure Case #1

Kodak: The Camera Giant That Refused to Change

What Went Wrong

  • • Invented digital camera in 1975 but BURIED it
  • • Protected film revenue instead of innovating
  • • Ignored the digital photography revolution
  • • Filed for bankruptcy in 2012

What They Should Have Done

  • • Embraced their own invention early
  • • Transitioned from film to digital services
  • • Built digital ecosystem like Apple later did
  • • Disrupted themselves like Netflix

Lesson: Protecting your current revenue at the cost of innovation = slow death.

SCRIPT: "Now, failure stories. Kodak — one of the most painful examples sa business history. Did you know, KODAK invented the digital camera in 1975? YES! They invented it! But what did they do? They BURIED it. Why? Because their profit was in film. Nag-think sila, 'Kung mag-release mi og digital camera, mamatay ang film business namo.' So they hid the technology. Meanwhile, other companies developed digital cameras. By the time Kodak tried to catch up, too late na. 2012, nag-file sila og bankruptcy. The lesson is clear: if you protect your current revenue at the expense of innovation, you will die slowly. Never be afraid to disrupt yourself."
Failure Case #2

Nokia: From Mobile King to Irrelevant

  • 2007: Nokia had 50% global market share in mobile phones
  • iPhone launched: Nokia dismissed it as "niche product"
  • 2010: Still using outdated Symbian OS while Android grew
  • 2013: Sold mobile division to Microsoft for $7.2B
  • CEO's famous words: "We didn't do anything wrong, but somehow we lost."
SCRIPT: "Nokia. In 2007, sila ang HARI sa mobile phones — 50% market share worldwide! When iPhone launched in 2007, Nokia dismissed it. 'That's a niche product,' they said. They stuck with Symbian OS while Android exploded. By 2013, forced sila mag-sell sa mobile division nila to Microsoft for $7.2 billion. Ang CEO nag-ingon: 'We didn't do anything wrong, but somehow we lost.' Class, that statement itself shows the problem — they thought doing NOTHING wrong was enough. But in tech, staying still IS going backward. You have to ACTIVELY innovate. Complacency is the enemy of progress."
Failure Case #3

Toys "R" Us: E-Commerce Too Late

The Failure

Outsourced e-commerce to Amazon in 2000. When they tried to build their own platform in 2004, it was buggy and slow. Customers already used Amazon. Filed bankruptcy in 2017.

The Lesson

Never outsource your CORE digital capability to a competitor. If you hand over your customer relationship to another platform, you lose control of your business.

Key Takeaway: Digital presence is not optional — it's a CORE business function, not something to outsource.

SCRIPT: "Toys R Us — the biggest toy retailer in the world. In 2000, instead of building their own e-commerce, nag-outsource sila to Amazon. Big mistake. They trained Amazon's customers to buy toys sa Amazon! When they finally tried to build their own platform in 2004, buggy ug slow. Customers already comfortable sa Amazon. By 2017, bankrupt na sila. The lesson: NEVER outsource your core digital capability to a competitor. Your digital presence is a CORE business function. Same sa accounting firms karon — if you don't have digital services, you're at risk. Your competitors who do will take your clients."

Change Management

Managing Resistance to Change

  • Communicate WHY: People resist what they don't understand
  • Involve employees early: Co-creation reduces resistance
  • Train extensively: Fear comes from lack of skills
  • Celebrate wins: Show progress, reward adoption
  • Support continuously: Help desk, champions, feedback loops
SCRIPT: "Since resistance is the #1 killer of DT, let's talk about managing it. Five strategies. First, COMMUNICATE WHY — explain to employees why we're changing. People resist what they don't understand. 'Kini para sa improvement, dili para tangtangon ka sa trabaho.' Second, INVOLVE EMPLOYEES EARLY — ask their input, make them part of the solution. Third, TRAIN EXTENSIVELY — most resistance comes from fear of 'dili ko kahibalo mag-gamit ani.' Give them training. Fourth, CELEBRATE WINS — when something works, announce it! Reward early adopters. Fifth, SUPPORT CONTINUOUSLY — have a help desk, digital champions in each department, feedback loops. These strategies, critical for any DT initiative."
Philippine Context

DT Challenges in Philippine Organizations

Common Challenges

• Limited IT budget (especially SMEs)
• Digital skills gap in workforce
• Internet connectivity issues in provinces
• Preference for traditional/paper processes
• Regulatory uncertainty (new tech laws)

Opportunities

• Young, tech-savvy population (median age 25)
• Growing fintech ecosystem
• Government push (e-Gov, DICT programs)
• BPO industry expertise
• Mobile-first market (high smartphone penetration)

SCRIPT: "Specifically sa Philippines, unsa ang challenges? Limited budget — especially SMEs, dili nila afford mahal nga systems. Digital skills gap — daghan pa ang dili tech-literate sa workforce. Internet connectivity — lahi ang experience sa Manila vs provinces. Preference for paper — 'mas safe og physical.' Regulatory uncertainty — new laws about data privacy, e-commerce, still evolving. BUT there are huge OPPORTUNITIES! Young population — median age 25, tech-savvy. Growing fintech. Government pushing e-Gov. BPO expertise — we have world-class IT talent. Mobile-first — almost everyone has a smartphone. So ang future sa Philippine DT, bright. Ang question is SPEED — how fast can we close the gap?"
