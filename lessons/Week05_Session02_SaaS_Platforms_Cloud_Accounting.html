Warm-up

What does a SaaS user actually control?

Users

Invitations, roles, MFA, inactive accounts

Configuration

Chart of accounts, tax codes, approval rules

Data

Accuracy, retention, classification, exports

Integrations

Bank feeds, POS, payroll, APIs

Evidence

Logs, reports, reconciliations, review notes

Vendor relationship

Contracts, support, SLA, exit planning

SCRIPT: “SaaS removes infrastructure work, but it does not remove accounting controls. The customer still owns users, configurations, data quality, integrations, evidence, and vendor management.”

Navigation Walkthrough

Generic SaaS control tour

  1. Confirm organization and reporting period
  2. Open Settings → Users and Roles
  3. Review connected apps and bank feeds
  4. Open Chart of Accounts and tax settings
  5. Create a test transaction
  6. Approve, post, reconcile, and report
  7. Export evidence and log the test result
SCRIPT: “This is our navigation pattern. We start with context and controls before entering transactions. When demonstrating software, say what you are testing and what evidence proves success.”
User Administration

Roles are accounting controls

SCRIPT: “Segregation of duties must survive the move to SaaS. Do not give everyone admin access because it is convenient. Convenience is not a control.”
Configuration

Chart of Accounts: clean structure, better reporting

  • Use consistent account names and numbering
  • Separate natural accounts from dimensions such as branch or project
  • Document who can create or change accounts
  • Map imported transactions to the correct accounts
  • Review inactive and duplicate accounts before migration

Bisaya reminder: “Kung gubot ang Chart of Accounts, gubot pud ang reports.”

SCRIPT: “Cloud does not fix poor accounting design. Clean the Chart of Accounts first. Decide what belongs in accounts and what belongs in dimensions such as branch, department, or project.”

SCRIPT: “SaaS helps automate workflows, but we still need control design. Notice each step has a system action and a control. The goal is not just speed; it is reliable, reviewable processing.”
Bank Feeds

Automated bank reconciliation: powerful but not automatic truth

  • Match imported bank transactions to ledger entries
  • Use rules carefully; rules can misclassify recurring payments
  • Review unmatched and unusual items daily or weekly
  • Protect bank connection credentials and scopes
  • Document review and exception resolution
SCRIPT: “Bank feeds save time, but they do not replace review. An automated rule can be wrong consistently. Accountants must inspect exceptions, unusual amounts, new payees, and unmatched transactions.”
Application Walkthrough

Test transaction: from invoice to report

  1. Create a test customer and invoice
  2. Apply tax and due date
  3. Submit for approval
  4. Record a simulated receipt
  5. Reconcile to a test bank line
  6. Run A/R aging and sales report
  7. Compare report totals with source entries
SCRIPT: “We will use a controlled test transaction, not live client data. The walkthrough ends in a report because accounting systems are valuable only when transactions become reliable information.”

APIs connect SaaS platforms, but every connection creates a control surface.

Data Quality

Five checks before trusting imported data

Completeness

Did all expected records arrive?

Accuracy

Do values match the source?

Validity

Do records follow required formats and rules?

Uniqueness

Were duplicates created?

Timeliness

Did data arrive when needed?

Reconciliation

Do control totals agree across systems?

SCRIPT: “Data quality is not an IT-only concern. Accountants understand control totals, reconciliations, exceptions, and cutoffs. Apply those skills to integrated data.”
Vendor Controls

What to ask a SaaS vendor

  • Where is customer data stored and processed?
  • What certifications and independent assurance reports are available?
  • How are access, encryption, backups, and incidents handled?
  • Can the customer export data in a usable format?
  • What happens to data after contract termination?
  • How are product changes communicated and tested?
SCRIPT: “Vendor questions should produce evidence. Ask for contracts, reports, policies, and testable commitments. Marketing language is not a control.”
Local Context

SaaS and Philippine compliance

  • Confirm support for Philippine tax and reporting needs
  • Check Data Privacy Act responsibilities and breach processes
  • Document data processing roles and third-party providers
  • Keep evidence for BIR, audit, and management review
  • Train users on phishing, passwords, and data sharing

Local fit means legal, operational, language, support, and connectivity fit.

SCRIPT: “A global SaaS tool may still need local configuration, tax support, and privacy review. Do not assume a vendor’s country of origin answers Philippine compliance questions.”

Recap

What good SaaS adoption looks like

  • Clear business need and success measures
  • Role design and MFA before broad rollout
  • Clean data and tested migration
  • Controlled integrations and reconciliations
  • Vendor evidence, exit plan, and user training
SCRIPT: “Good SaaS adoption is disciplined. Start with need, design controls, test data, monitor integrations, manage the vendor, and train people. The application is only one part of the system.”
Quiz

Week 5 Session 2 Quiz

1. Name four areas the customer still controls in SaaS.
2. Why are roles and segregation of duties important in cloud accounting?
3. Describe two controls for automated bank feeds.
4. What are three data-quality checks for an integration?
5. Give three questions to ask a SaaS vendor before adoption.
SCRIPT: “Ten minutes. Use accounting examples, not generic definitions.”
Answer Key

Quiz answers and rationales

  • 1. Users, roles, configuration, data governance, integrations, evidence, or vendor management. Rationale: SaaS does not remove customer accountability.
  • 2. Roles limit unauthorized preparation, approval, posting, or deletion. Rationale: segregation reduces fraud and error risk.
  • 3. Review unmatched items, monitor rules, protect credentials, reconcile control totals, document exceptions. Rationale: automation needs review.
  • 4. Completeness, accuracy, validity, uniqueness, timeliness, reconciliation. Rationale: imported data must be reliable before reporting.
  • 5. Storage location, security evidence, incident process, export capability, termination, support, change management. Rationale: vendor risk is part of system risk.
SCRIPT: “The strongest answers connect SaaS features to controls. That is the accounting information systems mindset.”
