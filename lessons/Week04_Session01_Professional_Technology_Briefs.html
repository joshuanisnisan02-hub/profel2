What is a
Technology Brief?

A concise professional document that informs and recommends

Definition

Technology Brief = Professional Recommendation Document

A technology brief is a short (1-3 page) document that summarizes a technology, evaluates its relevance to an organization, and provides a clear recommendation for action.

Think of it as: A decision-making tool for busy executives who need to understand technology quickly without becoming experts themselves.

SCRIPT: "A Technology Brief is a SHORT professional document — 1-3 pages only. Its purpose: summarize a technology, evaluate its relevance, and recommend action. Your AUDIENCE is busy executives or managers who don't have time to research themselves. They need YOU to do the research ug give them a clear 'here's what you should do and why.' Think of it like a doctor's prescription — they don't explain all the pharmacology, they tell you what to take ug why. Same energy. Concise, informed, actionable."
Types

Types of Technology Briefs

Trend Scan Brief

"Here's a new technology trend and what it means for us"

Example: Brief on Generative AI impact on accounting firms

Comparative Evaluation Brief

"We compared 3 tools and here's our pick"

Example: Comparing cloud accounting platforms for the firm

Implementation Recommendation Brief

"We should adopt this technology and here's how"

Example: Proposing RPA adoption for invoice processing

For this course: You'll write a Comparative Evaluation Brief — comparing 2 IS tools and recommending one.

SCRIPT: "Three types of tech briefs you'll encounter. TREND SCAN — 'Here's a new tech trend, here's what it means for us.' Example: writing about how GenAI might disrupt accounting. COMPARATIVE EVALUATION — 'We compared options and here's our recommendation.' You'll write this type — comparing 2 tools. IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION — 'We should adopt this, here's the plan.' More detailed, includes timeline ug budget. For your assignment, Comparative Evaluation — simpler but still professional. The Executive Summary

How to Write a Killer Executive Summary

Bad Example ❌

"This report aims to present an overview of various cloud accounting software options available in the market today. The researcher conducted extensive analysis of multiple platforms and their features. The findings suggest that several options exist with different pricing models..."

Problem: Vague, no recommendation, academic tone, wastes time

Good Example ✅

"Our firm needs cloud accounting software to replace the outdated desktop system by Q2 2026. We evaluated Xero, QuickBooks Online, and Zoho Books against 6 criteria weighted for our export clients' needs. Recommendation: Adopt Xero (₱3,200/month) for its superior multi-currency handling and 1,000+ integrations. ROI payback: 4 months through time savings."

Why it works: Problem → what we did → recommendation → ROI. Done in 4 sentences.

SCRIPT: "The Executive Summary makes or breaks your brief. Look at the BAD example — vague, academic, says nothing. A manager reads this and thinks 'Get to the point!' The GOOD example: immediately states the problem (need cloud accounting), what we did (evaluated 3 options against 6 criteria), the recommendation (Xero, specific price), ug the ROI (payback in 4 months). Four sentences. That's it. The executive knows EVERYTHING they need. If they want details, they read further. But many won't — they trust your analysis. THAT'S the power of a good executive summary. Practice writing these until they're crisp ug clear."
Writing Quality

Professional Writing Standards

DO ✅

• Use active voice ("We recommend...")
• Be specific (numbers, dates, prices)
• Use bullet points for easy scanning
• Include tables for comparisons
• Cite credible sources
• Proofread for grammar/spelling
• Keep sentences short (max 20 words)

DON'T ❌

• Use academic/flowery language
• Be vague ("some tools are better")
• Write long paragraphs
• Include irrelevant background
• Use first person excessively
• Make claims without evidence
• Submit without reviewing

Golden Rule: If you can say it in 10 words, don't use 20. Brevity = professionalism.

SCRIPT: "Professional writing standards — different from academic essays! DO: active voice, specific data, bullet points, tables, citations, proofread, short sentences. DON'T: flowery language, vague statements, long paragraphs, irrelevant filler. Golden Rule: if it can be said in 10 words, USE 10. Not 20. Executives equate brevity with competence. If your brief is 5 pages when it should be 2, it signals you can't organize your thoughts. Practice being concise. It's harder than being wordy, but it's the mark of a professional."

Step 1

Workshop Step 1: Define Your Context (5 min)

Answer these questions on your paper before writing:

  • What category? (e.g., cloud accounting, project management)
  • Which 2 tools? (specific names)
  • Who is your audience? (e.g., "small accounting firm owner in GenSan")
  • What problem are they solving? (be specific)
  • What constraints exist? (budget, team size, skills level)

Example: Category: Cloud Accounting. Tools: Xero vs Zoho Books. Audience: Owner of 3-person accounting firm in GenSan. Problem: Replace manual Excel bookkeeping. Budget: ₱3,000/month max.

SCRIPT: "Okay class, let's start. Step 1: define your context. On your paper, answer these 5 questions. What category? Which tools? Who is your audience — be SPECIFIC, not just 'a business.' What problem? What constraints? Give yourselves a realistic scenario. The example: comparing Xero vs Zoho Books for a small GenSan accounting firm wanting to replace Excel. Budget cap ₱3,000/month. That context makes the evaluation FOCUSED ug relevant. 5 minutes. Write your answers now."
Step 2

Workshop Step 2: Define Criteria & Weights (10 min)

Based on your audience's needs, choose 4-6 criteria and assign weights (must total 100%).

Sample CriteriaWhen to Weight HIGH
Functionality/FeaturesWhen specific features are critical for the job
Cost (TCO)When budget is limited (SMEs, startups)
Ease of UseWhen users are not tech-savvy
Integration/APIWhen connecting to other systems is needed
ScalabilityWhen the business is growing rapidly
Security/ComplianceWhen handling sensitive data (financial, health)
Vendor SupportWhen team has limited internal IT expertise
Mobile AccessWhen staff work in the field or remotely
SCRIPT: "Step 2: criteria ug weights. Based on YOUR audience's situation, pick 4-6 criteria. Then weight them. If your audience is budget-limited, cost gets high weight. If they handle sensitive data, security gets high weight. If they're not tech-savvy, ease of use is critical. JUSTIFY your weights — 'Cost is weighted at 30% because the client is a micro-business with limited budget.' 10 minutes. Define your criteria, assign weights. Remember: total must be 100%."
Step 3

Workshop Step 3: Research & Score (15 min)

Research Sources

• Official vendor websites (features, pricing)
• G2.com / Capterra.com (user reviews)
• Tech blogs (PCMag, TechRadar reviews)
• YouTube (tool walkthroughs/tutorials)
• Reddit / forums (real user experiences)

Scoring Guide

5 = Excellent, fully meets/exceeds need
4 = Good, meets need with minor gaps
3 = Adequate, basic coverage
2 = Poor, significant gaps
1 = Unacceptable, fails to meet need

Use your phones to research now! Visit the vendor websites, check reviews. Score each tool on each criterion.

SCRIPT: "Step 3: research ug score! Use your phones NOW. Go to the vendor websites — check features, pricing. Go to G2.com or Capterra — check user reviews. Score each tool 1-5 on each of your criteria. Be fair — don't bias toward the tool you already like. Base it on EVIDENCE. Note down specific evidence for your scores: 'Scored 4 on ease of use because G2 average rating is 4.3/5 with 89% recommending.' 15 minutes of research. Go!"
Step 4

Workshop Step 4: Draft Your Brief (20 min)

Write your brief following the template. Start with the Executive Summary (write it LAST but put it FIRST).

Writing Order (Easy Method)

1. Write Sections 3-8 first (body)
2. Write Section 2 LAST (Executive Summary)
3. Add Section 1 (title) and 9 (sources)

Why? The exec summary is easier to write AFTER you know what you're recommending.

Time Allocation

• Business Context: 2 min
• Options Description: 3 min
• Criteria & Scoring Table: 5 min
• Recommendation: 3 min
• Next Steps: 2 min
• Executive Summary: 3 min
• Review & Polish: 2 min

SCRIPT: "Step 4: WRITE! Pro tip — write the Executive Summary LAST even though it appears first in the document. Why? Because it's easier to summarize after you know your recommendation. Start with the body: context, options, criteria table, scoring, recommendation, next steps. Then circle back ug write the exec summary. Time allocation on the right. 20 minutes total.
