01
What cloud computing really means

On-demand access to shared computing resources over a network, with rapid provisioning and usage-based measurement.

SCRIPT: “Cloud means computing resources available when needed, over a network, with measured usage. Think of electricity: you do not own the power plant; you consume what you need and pay based on use. Same idea, but for servers, storage, databases, and applications.”
Core Concept

Cloud is a service model, not a location

A server can be physically near you and still be managed as a cloud service. The important question is: who provisions, manages, secures, and bills the resource?

Bisaya shortcut: “Dili importante asa ang server. Importante kinsa ang nag-manage ug unsaon nato pag-access.”

SCRIPT: “Common misconception: cloud means the server is somewhere else. Not always. A private cloud can be inside an organization. What makes it cloud-like is the service experience: on-demand access, shared resources, automation, elasticity, and measurement.”
Characteristics

Five characteristics of cloud computing

On-demand self-service

Users provision resources without waiting for manual IT setup.

Broad network access

Services are reachable through standard devices and networks.

Resource pooling

Provider resources serve many customers using isolation controls.

Rapid elasticity

Capacity expands or contracts as demand changes.

Measured service

Usage is monitored, controlled, and billed transparently.

Accounting link

Usage data supports budgeting, cost allocation, and audit evidence.

SCRIPT: “These five characteristics are your checklist. If a vendor claims cloud but cannot explain elasticity or measured usage, ask more questions. For accountants, measured service is especially important because cloud costs may move with usage.”
Service Models

IaaS, PaaS, SaaS: who manages what?

ModelYou receiveYou manageExample
IaaSVirtual servers, storage, networksOS, apps, data, configurationsAWS EC2
PaaSManaged runtime and development platformApplication code and dataAzure App Service
SaaSFinished applicationUsers, settings, data governanceQuickBooks Online
SCRIPT: “Remember the responsibility line. IaaS gives you building blocks. PaaS gives you a place to build. SaaS gives you the finished application. For AIS professionals, SaaS is the most visible, but understanding the lower layers helps you evaluate security and vendor responsibility.”
Deployment Models

Public, private, hybrid, community

Public cloud

Shared provider infrastructure. Flexible and fast, but governance must be designed carefully.

Private cloud

Dedicated environment for one organization. More control, usually more cost.

Hybrid cloud

Combination of on-premise/private and public resources connected by integration.

Community cloud

Shared by organizations with common regulatory or mission needs.

Accounting decision

Choose based on data sensitivity, compliance, skills, and total cost, not fashion.

Local reality

Hybrid is often practical for PH organizations with legacy systems.

SCRIPT: “Public is not automatically insecure, and private is not automatically secure. Security depends on design, controls, people, and monitoring. Many Philippine organizations use hybrid because they cannot replace legacy systems overnight.”

Accounting Use Case

Why firms move from desktop to cloud accounting

  • Multiple users and branches can work from one source
  • Bank feeds reduce repetitive encoding
  • Updates and backups are managed continuously
  • Auditors can receive controlled access
  • Reports are available closer to real time
SCRIPT: “The business case is not ‘cloud is trendy.’ The case is collaboration, timeliness, reduced manual work, and controlled access. But always balance those benefits with privacy, internet dependency, and vendor risk.”
Cost Model

Cloud pricing: subscription is not the whole story

Cost areaWhat to inspect
SubscriptionPer user, per feature, per month or year
UsageStorage, transactions, API calls, data transfer
ImplementationMigration, configuration, integrations, training
ComplianceAdditional logging, retention, regional requirements
ExitData export, migration, contract termination

Decision rule: compare three-year total cost, not the first monthly invoice.

SCRIPT: “This is where accounting thinking helps. Do not compare only monthly subscription prices. Include implementation, integrations, training, compliance, and exit. A cheap tool that creates manual work may be expensive in labor.”

02
Shared responsibility

Cloud security is shared between provider and customer. The split changes by service model.

SCRIPT: “Cloud provider ang responsible sa some controls, customer sa others. In SaaS, the provider handles more infrastructure, but the customer still controls identities, data, settings, and user behavior. Shared responsibility does not mean shared blame; it means clear ownership.”
Security

Physical facilitiesData center access and powerVendor due diligence

SCRIPT: “During an audit interview, ask: who owns this control, what evidence exists, and how often is it reviewed? That is much stronger than simply asking whether the system is in the cloud.”
Reliability

Availability, backup, and disaster recovery

Availability

How often the service is usable. Check SLA, maintenance windows, and incident history.

Backup

Copies of data. Ask frequency, retention, encryption, and restore testing.

Disaster recovery

How service and data are restored after major disruption.

RPO

Recovery Point Objective: acceptable amount of lost data.

RTO

Recovery Time Objective: acceptable time to restore operations.

Audit evidence

Request reports, test results, and incident records, not promises only.

SCRIPT: “RPO asks how much data we can afford to lose. RTO asks how long we can afford to be offline. For payroll or tax filing, these numbers may be very different from a small internal app. Always match recovery requirements to business impact.”
Migration

Moving an accounting system to the cloud

  • Discover and clean the source data before migration
  • Map accounts, customers, vendors, tax codes, and opening balances
  • Define roles and approval workflows before go-live
  • Run a pilot with a small dataset or branch
  • Reconcile old and new reports during parallel operation
  • Document cutover, rollback, and support procedures
SCRIPT: “Migration is not upload-and-pray. Data quality first. Map fields carefully. Pilot before full cutover. Keep a reconciliation period so finance can confirm opening balances and reports. Document rollback because surprises happen.”
Application Walkthrough

Generic SaaS navigation pattern

  1. Sign in and verify the correct organization
  2. Open Settings → Users/Roles
  3. Connect bank or import a controlled sample
  4. Review Chart of Accounts
  5. Create a test invoice and approval workflow
  6. Run a report and export evidence
SCRIPT: “For any SaaS demonstration, follow this navigation pattern. Start with the organization and permissions. Then inspect data connections, chart of accounts, workflows, reports, and export. Dili ta mag-click randomly. We follow a control-minded walkthrough.”
Mini Case

Case: A three-branch accounting firm

The firm uses spreadsheets, emails attachments, and a desktop ledger. Partners want remote access. Staff worry about internet outages and losing control of client data.

Business need

Shared records, faster review, controlled client access.

Cloud benefit

Centralized access, automated updates, collaboration.

Risk

Weak permissions, poor migration, vendor dependency.

Recommendation

Pilot SaaS with MFA, role design, backups, and an offline contingency plan.

SCRIPT: “A good recommendation is balanced. We do not say ‘move everything immediately.’ We recommend a pilot, controls, and a contingency plan. That is the difference between technology enthusiasm and professional systems analysis.”

Professional Judgment

When cloud may not be the best answer

  • Connectivity is unreliable and no continuity plan exists
  • Data residency or regulatory needs cannot be met
  • Vendor cannot provide adequate security evidence
  • Migration cost exceeds realistic business value
  • Organization lacks change capacity and support resources

“Cloud-first” is a starting hypothesis, not an automatic conclusion.

SCRIPT: “Cloud-first does not mean cloud-only. If connectivity is unreliable, if vendor evidence is weak, or if the economics do not work, recommend another path. Professional judgment means knowing when not to adopt.”
Recap

Five takeaways

  • Cloud is on-demand, networked, pooled, elastic, and measured.
  • IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS shift responsibility differently.
  • Public, private, hybrid, and community deployments serve different contexts.
  • Security, backup, recovery, identity, and data governance remain customer concerns.
  • Evaluate cloud with business value, TCO, controls, and implementation readiness.
SCRIPT: “If you remember only five things, remember these. Cloud is a service model. Responsibility shifts but never disappears. TCO matters. Controls matter. And the right answer depends on context.”

Answer Key

Quiz answers and rationales

  • 1. On-demand network access to shared computing resources. Characteristics may include self-service, broad access, pooling, elasticity, measured service. Rationale: shows the student understands cloud beyond ‘online storage.’
  • 2. IaaS provides infrastructure, PaaS a development platform, SaaS a finished application. Rationale: responsibility changes by model.
  • 3. Include implementation, training, usage, integrations, compliance, and exit costs. Rationale: monthly price can hide TCO.
  • 4. Provider supports identity features, but customer manages users, MFA, roles, data classification, and sharing. Rationale: shared responsibility requires customer controls.
  • 5. Any two justified controls: MFA, role review, backup/restore test, migration reconciliation, vendor due diligence, rollback plan. Rationale: controls reduce implementation and operating risk.
